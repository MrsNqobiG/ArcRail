What Arc is, in plain terms

Arc is a payments network run by Circle, the company behind the USDC digital dollar. Money moves between digital wallets in under a second, fees are small and predictable, and a payment, once confirmed, is final. Every transaction is public, so no customer names or personal details ever go onto the network.

SettledConfirmed final, then the reserve is turned into a payment

Amber steps are control points. If anything is unclear at any step, the money stops and a person is alerted. Nothing moves on a guess.

The main parts

Stays as is

Core banking system

Still the official record of customers, balances, checks and the accounts ledger.

New

Arc connector

Watches the network, books payments into the bank system, and runs each payment step by step.

New

Signing vault

Holds the keys. Signs only payments a person approved, within set limits, and refuses anything else.

New

Independent watchdog

Separately checks that the network, the connector and the bank system agree to the cent. If not, it stops all payments.

New

Own network nodes

The bank's own view of Arc, compared against a second source so a faulty feed can't fool us.

Existing services

Compliance checks

Sanctions screening and the travel-rule data exchange required by South African law.

Safety nets built into the design

1

Books always balance

Every movement is checked so the network, the connector and the bank system agree exactly.

2

Never paid twice

Every payment carries a unique reference, so retries can't duplicate it.

3

Stops when unsure

Any mismatch pauses outgoing payments and alerts a person. Restarting needs two people.

4

Two-person approval

Payments need a second person's approval with a physical security key.

5

Spending limits in the vault

Per-payment and daily caps, including fees, are enforced where the keys are.

6

Privacy by default

No personal information on the public network or in system logs.

What we need from leadership

Decision

How the bank holds the keys: its own secure hardware, or a regulated custodian.

Decision

Strategy: the central bank has signalled caution on US-dollar stablecoins for local payments. Do we stay USD-first, focus on cross-border, or plan for another asset?

Decision

Which core banking system and team we integrate with, so the connector can be wired to the real system.

Before real money

Eight sign-offs: regulator authorisation, a travel-rule test with another provider, an exchange-control legal opinion, an independent security review, a key ceremony and drill, a privacy impact assessment, bank-partner approval, and a staged rollout plan.
