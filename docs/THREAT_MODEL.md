## Independent monitor (proposed ADR-008, a decision for G1)
Threats whose attacker is **a compromised adapter** (T-S1, T-T2, T-E1, T-E2) can't be detected by code running inside the adapter, because the attacker could change the detection's scope or drop its PAUSE (threat-model R5 D1). The design therefore splits reconciliation and detection (U12) into an **independent monitor**:
- **Separate trust domain:** its own VM and network segment, its own service identity and credentials. Its **reference** inputs (read-only) are:
  - own nodes;
  - the CBS read operations `getBalancesAsOf`, `listJournals`, `listPayoutInstructions`, `listApprovals`, `listIssuedAddresses` and `listCaseDispositions`, plus the CBS's monitoring and screening records (CF-5(f), (h), (i));
  - **Treasury's bank-owned wallet list** (DR-24);
  - the **extended public key (xpub)** for bank-derived addresses, from the key ceremony (DR-04);
  - Security's artefacts: canary mappings, the honeytoken list, **the WORM anchor** (the adapter publishes its outbox and audit chain head every hour to Security-owned write-once storage with object lock; owner: Security), and **the checkers' FIDO2 credential registry** (owner: Security, from the identity provider);
  - identity-provider assertions for unpause actions (DR-08);
  - **owner PAUSE requests:** a named owner (Treasury, Security, Compliance, Engineering) may request PAUSE on the monitor's authenticated operator channel, signed with that person's identity-provider identity. **One owner is enough to stop**; unpausing still needs two people (DR-08). Defined in ADR-008 (operator channel);
  - **the signer's caps and fee limits** (ADR-001 duties 3 and 6): the per-move and daily move caps, duty 3's daily cap, the `maxPriorityFeePerGas` ceiling and the worst-case-fee (`gasLimit × maxFeePerGas`) ceiling. Owned by Treasury with Risk, owner-signed and version-pinned (T-T6), and published with Treasury's wallet list (DR-01);
  - **the screening service**, called directly with the canary address (DR-17), and the CBS's screening records (DR-28);
  - **the database engine's row-level audit log** (owner: Security; DR-26). The monitor reads the registry under **its own database identity**, never the adapter's.

  It reads adapter records **only as the subject under check** (ADR-008 per-detection input map). It holds a copy of address↔account links under the same controls as U5 (L-2).
- **Signing log:** the signer pushes each `(payloadHash, nonce, txHash, instructionId)` entry directly to the monitor.
- **Enforcement the adapter can't bypass:** the monitor issues a short-lived **attestation**, `ALL_CLEAR` or `PAUSE`, signed with its own key. The signer holds that public key in its own trust store, and **refuses to sign without a fresh `ALL_CLEAR`** (no older than `A_attest`, proposed 60 s). PAUSE and a missing or stale attestation both stop signing. This is a dead-man switch: monitor downtime fails closed.
- **Executors (MC-40(e)).** Every detection, or each named part of a detection, is run by exactly one of these. Detections of **adapter compromise** run only in the monitor and end in a monitor-issued PAUSE.
  - **Monitor:** DR-01, DR-03 (audit re-verification), DR-04, DR-05 (chain-head check against the WORM anchor), DR-06, DR-07, DR-08, DR-10, DR-11, DR-12, DR-13, DR-14, DR-15, DR-16 part (2), DR-17, DR-19 part (1), DR-21, DR-22, DR-23, DR-24, DR-25, DR-26 part (1), DR-28, DR-29, RD-01 (its own check), RD-03.
  - **CI and deployment pipeline (owner: Engineering), before every deploy:** DR-19 part (2), including its deploy block. It catches a logging bug in code about to be deployed, not adapter compromise, so it doesn't need to run in the monitor.
  - **Signer:** the sign-time checks (T-T1 preventive, T-E5 shapes, MC-24 replay, ADR-001 duties).
  - **Adapter:** DR-02 (quorum, against lying RPCs, not adapter compromise); DR-16 part (1) (the adapter sends, so only it sees send results; a raised fee floor is a chain change, not adapter compromise); DR-18 (pre-sign travel-rule digest, against the provider, not adapter compromise); RD-01 (its pre-posting gate).
  - **Security egress proxy:** DR-20, DR-26 part (2).
  - **Security canary system:** DR-09.
  - **Deployment tooling at node and adapter start:** DR-27.
- **Ageing outside the adapter (ADR-008 attestation preconditions; DR-29):** the monitor ages, from **its own finality block** and never from an adapter timestamp:
  - every claimed in-flight item;
  - **every final status-1 payout and case-return transaction in the signing log**, whether or not the adapter claims it. Its settlement (payout) or its T9 or T10 (case return) must be joined within `A_post` (CONTRACT §5.8).

  An item older than its CONTRACT §5.8 age blocks `ALL_CLEAR`. A compromised adapter therefore can't keep a genuine final payout or case return "in flight", or leave it unclaimed, forever.
- **Monitor → adapter `RESUME` channel (ADR-008):** after a two-person unpause, the monitor sends a signed `RESUME` to the adapter over its own mutually authenticated link. The adapter clears its PAUSE flag only on that `RESUME` plus a recorded resolution of its own condition.
- **Fail-closed actions** of monitor detections: the monitor issues **PAUSE** through the attestation, so the signer stops. An item-level QUARANTINE requested by the monitor is **recorded by the monitor** and, if it isn't honoured, escalates to PAUSE.
- **Residual (G1 residual 8):** a **simultaneous** compromise of the adapter and the monitor; of the signer; or of an artefact owner (for example Treasury for its list and caps) plus the adapter (T-T6). Mitigated by separate admin identities and separate change control for the three trust domains.

It starts from `m = ⌊W/k⌋`, where `W` is that log's value. The dust, `W mod k`, stays in D (CONTRACT §5.3). From `m`, subtract `R` for every T9 or T10 bound to the item as above. T2, T8 and `unid` net the item's T9s; T11 nets its T9s and T10s. A fully returned item (expected 0) has no such journal.<br>• **Address and account.** T2's `refs.address` equals the log's `to`, and its G1 account is the one the CBS issued that address to (`listIssuedAddresses`). T8's log `to` is on Treasury's list. `unid`'s log `to` is in **neither** `listIssuedAddresses` nor Treasury's list, because CONTRACT posts `unid` only for a `to` with no mapping (§5.3). T11's G1 account is the `accountRef` of the `ASSIGN` that `refs.assignRef` cites, on a case whose subject is the item. If the CBS's own record holds a later `ASSIGN`, `HOLD` or `RETURN` for the subject, that `ASSIGN` is superseded: the CBS refuses T11 with `NOT_PERMITTED`, a business refusal that sends the item back to SUSPENSE with a `POSTING_REJECTED` case. That supersession check is the CBS's (CONTRACT §3, Q-C19), because the monitor sees only journals that were posted.<br>Any mismatch → PAUSE.<br>**Healthy:** zero mismatches each cycle. **Re-trace** at p = 2, k = 10¹⁶, W = 10²⁰ wei, so m = 10,000:<br>• a partial return R = 2,500 (a bound T9) leaves T2 = 7,500, which matches;<br>• for an item with no mapping, the same return leaves `unid` = 7,500, and a further bound R = 500 from G4 (T10) leaves T11 = 7,000; both match;<br>• **cross-item** (threat-model R13 C1): item X has m = 10,000 and no return. Item Y has m = 6,000 and a genuine `RETURN` of R = 2,500. The adapter sends Y's return, withholds Y's T9, and posts a T9 of 2,500 citing a case it opened on X. That case has no `RETURN` disposition, so the T9 is unbound → PAUSE. Y's final case-return transaction also has no T9 within `A_post` → PAUSE. If the T9 cites Y's case instead, it nets only for Y, and T2_X = 7,500 ≠ 10,000 → PAUSE.<br>**Needs CF-5(f), CF-5(h), CF-5(i), and `listJournals` with `legs` and `refs`** (CONTRACT fix block I). CF-5(i)'s `listCaseDispositions` must also return each disposition's case subject (CF-38(d)). Until then residual 6 | CBS journals with legs and `refs`; CBS payout instructions and approvals; issuance record; `ASSIGN` and `RETURN` dispositions with their cases' subjects; Treasury's list; own log fetch and own receipts; signing log | (1) A compromised adapter settles customer C's payout against customer V's account → V's debit has no instruction for V → PAUSE.<br>(2) A settlement cites another customer's paid `txHash` → that transaction's signing-log `instructionId` differs → PAUSE.<br>(3) After a partial return the adapter posts T2 for `m` (contract R6 D1) → 10,000 ≠ 7,500 → PAUSE.<br>(4) One item's genuine case return is booked as a T9 on another item (threat-model R13 D1) → the T9 is unbound, or the first item's return is unjoined past `A_post` → PAUSE.<br>(5) A customer's deposit to an issued collection address is booked as `unid` into suspense → the log `to` is in `listIssuedAddresses` → PAUSE |

## Residual risks for the human at G1
1. **T-D5 (issuer freeze)** can't be mitigated technically. The bank accepts it or it doesn't.
2. **L-1 and L-5 (public flows)** are inherent until Arc privacy goes live (C-64).
3. **T-SC1** stays open until Q-T3 is answered. **T-SC4:** an arc-node image that is already tampered when its digest is approved passes DR-27; this stays open until Q-A16 (signed releases) or a reviewed source build (RISK_REGISTER RB-3).
4. **T-T1, T-E2 (approval part), T-R1 and DR-03** stay OPEN until the chosen CBS can produce a FIDO2 `checkerAssertion` over `payloadDigest` (CONTRACT §3, Q-C10). Until then a compromised adapter could forge an approval. The signer's **per-transaction and daily limits** cap the damage. The shape allow-list doesn't (a fabricated plain value send is an allowed shape), and nor does the destination allow-list, which the compromised adapter controls. The per-merchant destination allow-list is owned by merchant onboarding in the CBS (maker-checker) and is read by U7 from the CBS
5. **DR-18 (travel-rule tampering)** needs the CONTRACT clarification in CF-5(e). Until then T-B2 relies on [X] anchors only.
6. **DR-01, DR-04, DR-20 (address set), DR-21, DR-22, DR-23, DR-25, DR-28 and DR-29 can't run** until the CBS read operations in CF-5(f), CF-5(h) and CF-5(i) exist (RISK_REGISTER RB-7). **DR-04 also needs an exportable xpub** for the collection branch (Q-D3 for an HSM, Q-D1(h) for a custodian); it stays in this residual until those are answered. Until then:
   - a compromised orchestrator inventing an instruction (T-S1, RR-3 3a), replaying an approval, or swapping an account mapping (T-T2) **is not detected by any [R] check**, because DR-06 and DR-07 stay at residual 0 when the fabricated transaction is posted consistently (threat-model R5 D3);
   - only [X] customer statements and complaints remain;
   - the preventive signer limits cap the damage per transaction and per day.
7. **T-SC3 (prompt injection into the build agent)** is mitigated by human gates and fresh-context verifiers, but there is no automated detection. Accepted for a build-time-only risk.
8. **Simultaneous compromise of two trust domains** (adapter and monitor; the signer; or **an artefact owner, such as Treasury for its list and caps, plus the adapter**, T-T6) defeats the B9 design. Mitigated by separate admin identities and separate change control (proposed ADR-008).
9. **Registry theft (L-2)** is detected by DR-26 only once the database provides row-level read auditing (Q-D4). Until then: encryption at rest and access review (RISK_REGISTER RB-10).
10. **T-I2 (xpub disclosure)** has preventive controls only. The bank accepts it, or declines DR-04 and keeps the registry-only check DR-23.
11. **DR-16 depends on Q-A2.** If Arc silently drops below-floor transactions, a raised floor is seen only at `A_stuck` (30 min), not at send time.
12. **[X]-only windows:** a stale sanctions list between a publication and the next daily check (T-B1); a malicious dependency with no advisory yet, **or between an advisory and the next daily scan** (T-SC2); a quiet disclosure of secret artefacts until the next daily review (T-I3).
13. **Travel-rule data integrity at a compromised provider** (T-B2, DR-18 limit): a provider that reports the CBS's digest but sends altered data has no independent detection, **for unhosted wallets always**, and **for hosted CASPs until ADR-004 provides a counterparty acknowledgement** (tracked as **Q-D9** and LEDGER **CF-24**) (an echoed digest, checked per transfer by Compliance's travel-rule operations, with a mismatch → PAUSE request on the monitor's operator channel). RISK_REGISTER RB-6.
14. **Duplicate T6 (RISK_REGISTER 4b, RB-12).** An internal move posted twice moves value between G2 sub-accounts with no chain effect, so the aggregate identity (DR-06) stays at 0. Only RD-03 (RISK_REGISTER §RD, in the monitor) can catch it, and only once CF-5(b) makes the per-role check PAUSE instead of only diagnosing. Until then it is undetected. No value leaves the bank, but the G2 sub-account balances are misstated.
15. **A move strictly above the approval threshold without a checker (CF-37).** The signer needs a checker assertion for such a move (ADR-001 item 6), but no detection re-checks this after the fact. CONTRACT §5.6 defines no move-approval record (CF-15, Q-C16), so DR-01 has nothing to join the move to. A signer that skips the rule, through a bug or a compromise, is still bounded by Treasury's list and the move caps, which DR-01 re-checks, so no value leaves the bank. This closes when CONTRACT defines the move-approval record and DR-01 (and ADR-008's move rule) joins such moves to it.
